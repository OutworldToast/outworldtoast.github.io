Azuma's Sacrifice

I'm most proud of the work I've done on Azuma's Sacrifice, an action-platformer game we made with a group of 9 where I was the director.

As the leader, my main priorities were ensuring that we did plenty of playtesting, as well as keeping our vision for the game in one line.
I ran weekly playtests with other students of the course, as well as leading meetings for game and narrative design - trying to keep decision-making fast, while not compromising on reaching consensus.

Aside from leading the team, I also did QA (much to the chagrin of my fellow programmers) as well as implementing many of the core systems.

HackJack

When we first started work on Reel's Casino , we needed to make a 'casino game' that felt rigged to the player, while actually being well-balanced and strategic.

We started with a simple idea: what if you were playing a game of blackjack, but the dealer suddenly pulled out Uno cards? We came up with some basic game rules, then tried it out using a paper prototype. The immediate conclusion: the game was terribly balanced and the player always won.

I took it home and tried addressing the issues one by one - like how to keep the game random letting players win just off variance for example. Then, after implementing these changes and a bit more iteration with the group, we ended up in a few days with a game so fun we weren't doing our other coursework anymore.

While it still took a lot more playtesting and iteration, including on two different variants, it's a game I'm too happy with not to mention on it's own - even if it was only meant to be a part of Reel's Casino .

Reel's Casino

The first real game I worked on, together with 5 other GDS students with no prior experience in game dev.

During initial brainstorming, two of my groupmates pitched the idea of a game where you were trapped in a nightmare casino where you could not escape until you beat the rigged games. I liked the idea so much that I voted for it over my own idea, at which point we quickly designed HackJack and got to work turning it into a video game.

As head programmer and designer, I worked on implementing our designs and iterating based on our playtests. Additionally, I also wrote most of the dialogue.

While we're happy with what we've made, we believe there's still a lot of work to do on the game. We still try to tinker with it in our spare time, aiming for an eventual release.
